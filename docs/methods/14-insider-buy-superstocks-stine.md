# 14 — Insider Buy Superstocks (Jesse Stine: weekly 30-week MA breakout + 10-week "magic line")

*Practitioner write-up. Researched 2026-10-06 for swing-engine. Every number is tagged with where it came from:
"(primary)" = Stine's own book preview / site / documentation page; "(book excerpt)" = first-person chapter text
reproduced on the insiderbuysuperstocks.weebly.com promo site, whose chapter titles match the book's table of
contents but whose operator is not named (treat as near-primary); "(secondary)" = third-party summaries, reviews,
threads; "(unverified)" = could not be checked against a primary source in this run. The full book (2013, ~250
pages) was not read; pirated PDF copies that surface in search were deliberately not used.*

**One line.** A weekly-chart, low-priced, small-float "earnings winner" bought when a huge weekly volume thrust
lifts it out of a long base above its 30-week moving average at a ~45-degree angle, ideally with open-market insider
buying, then added to on low-volume pullbacks to its "magic line" (usually the 10-week MA) and sold into
extension (7–10 weeks after a thrust, ~9 months into the advance, parabolic/largest-range weeks, 60–100% above the
magic line) or on a weekly close below the magic line.

**Lineage.** The 30-week-MA breakout is Stan Weinstein's Stage-2 entry (*Secrets for Profiting in Bull and Bear
Markets*, 1988); the earnings-blowout + volume-breakout + small-float idea is O'Neil/CAN SLIM territory; the insider
angle is Seyhun/Lakonishok-style insider-purchase research. Do not confuse with Kenneth Fisher's *Super Stocks*
(1984), which is a price-to-sales value method. Stine himself frames it as "the absolute best fundamentals in
alignment with the very best technicals while trading at a distressed price" (Preface, primary).

### Market filter
No mechanical regime rule was found in primary text. What is stated:
- "A vast majority of stocks fall during bear markets. Superstocks are no exception." (primary, Assumptions)
- "I often stay in cash and step away from the market for long periods when stocks aren't acting right" (primary, Introduction).
- Contrarian sentiment / "global inflection point" calls (Appendix A; annual outlooks 2010–2013 such as "Do Absolutely Nothing" 2010) and a "friends and family" email-alert list (primary, preview).
- A "Canary in a Coalmine" indicator that "precedes market crashes within 6 days" (highlight #6, primary). **Its definition was not found in any accessible source — unverified.**

### Entry
"Top 5 must-haves" at the breakout week (book excerpt; enumerated identically by @bigjsbach, Aug 2018, secondary):
1. **Long, strong base**: "Stocks that have been basing for a year or more", or "eight weeks or more" of tight range after a decline (book excerpt). tonyg99's AOI checklist used "8 months of low-volume trading" (secondary).
2. **Weekly close above the 30-week moving average** (book excerpt; FWTV; TradingSkeptic). One TradingView author substituted a 20-week SMA (tonyg99, secondary) — an adaptation, not the book rule.
3. **Huge weekly volume thrust**: "**500% to 5,000%**" volume surge at the breakout (book excerpt); tonyg99 adds "sustained volume expansion for **two weeks** post-breakout" (secondary).
4. **High angle of attack** ≈ **45 degrees**: "a $4 stock breaking out … at a 45 degree angle would be trending to $6 or $7 within a couple of months; a 50%–75% short-term return" (book excerpt).
5. **Price under $15** (above).

Low-risk entry "Super Laws" (Ch. 9; summarised on weebly home):
- Buy **2–3 weeks after the earnings breakout**, following a test of the gap.
- Buy at the lower trendline during low-volume, narrow-range weeks.
- "Buy AGGRESSIVELY at the magic line, ideally on the lowest recent weekly volume."
- Magic line = the stock-specific n-week MA it respects; for the best performers the **10-week simple MA** (book excerpt). "Most Superstocks hit their magic line or 10 week moving average at least once every **10 to 12 weeks**" (book excerpt). @bigjsbach: "The author suggests 10 WMA but it is a subject of some experimenting" (secondary). Brad Jelinek (Jan 2026) describes it as whichever of the 10-week, 20-day or 50-day the stock keeps bouncing off (secondary).
- Worked example (FWTV, secondary): Dynamic Materials bought at **$4.45** while price consolidated and volume dried up; sold near **+500% in 8 weeks**.

### Stop
- Stine stresses cutting losses: "a MAJORITY of my trades didn't work out … as long as I cut my many losses short, my few winners carried my account" (primary, Introduction). His 2008 loss is attributed to "trading without stop losses" (FWTV, secondary). TradingReviewers lists "strict stop losses" among the book's laws (secondary).
- **No numeric stop percentage was found in an accessible source (unverified).** The rule-consistent mechanical stop is a **weekly close below the magic line** (listed as a sell signal on the weebly home summary) or, for a fresh breakout, a weekly close back below the 30-week MA / base top.

### Exits and targets
No price target; sell into strength by rule. "Where you sell a stock is 1,000 times more important than where you buy a stock" (Ch. 10, quoted by TradingReviewers, secondary). Sell signals (weebly home Lazy Man's Guide summary unless noted):
- **Time**: **7–10 weeks after the initial thrust**; **9 months into the advance** (secondary search summaries say "9–15 month advance from breakout" — unverified).
- **Price action**: parabolic surges; weekly gaps at new highs; the **largest weekly price range** of the advance; the **4th or 5th surge** off the magic line; a **weekly close below the magic line**.
- **Extension** (secondary, via Brad Jelinek's Jan 2026 sell-rule list and search summaries, not seen in primary text): ~**60% above** the magic line/10-week → lighten up unless it is the first advance; **>100% above** → expect sideways or down. FWTV: sold Dynamic Materials because "price moved way too far away from the 30 week moving average".
- **Fundamental / event**: "Sell secondary offerings/private placements without hesitation" (TradingSkeptic quoting the book); end of the earnings ramp; stock splits; insider selling; mainstream media headlines; message-board euphoria (weebly home).
- **Price level**: some stocks "must be sold at $25" (primary highlight; mechanism unverified).

### Position sizing
- Concentrated: the goal is "the one or two biggest future winners"; "Luck is all about following the **Kelly Criterion** … and betting big when all of the variables align" (primary, Introduction). No fraction or per-trade risk % found (unverified).
- Risk record (primary): early-career account drawdowns of **61%, 64%, 65%, 100%, 100% and 106%**; the book's editor (Tony Loton) reports his own portfolio falling "by as much as 50% (or even more)" en route to a 3,000% return. Stine: "It is now my mission to help you beat the odds without taking on the level of risk that I occasionally took on."

### Holding period
"Position Trader holding my stocks for weeks or months"; "My longest hold during my 28 month portfolio advance was **8 months**" (primary). Typical cycle in the examples: entry at a weekly buy point, exit 7–10 weeks after a thrust, re-entry at the next magic-line touch.

## Chart signatures
- **Base**: 8–12+ months of sideways, low-volume weekly bars; 30-week MA flattening, then turning up.
- **Breakout week**: a weekly bar closing above both the base top and the 30-week MA on volume 5–50× its recent weekly average, frequently the week of a blowout earnings report (often a gap).
- **Follow-through**: second week of expanded volume; price advancing at ~45°; Financial Wisdom TV calls the repeating pattern a "staircase" (thrust, sideways consolidation on shrinking volume, thrust).
- **Magic-line pullbacks**: every ~10–12 weeks the stock dips to its 10-week MA on the lowest weekly volume in weeks, then thrusts again.
- **Exhaustion**: largest weekly range of the move, weekly gap at new highs, 4th–5th thrust off the magic line, price 60–100%+ above the 10-week, an equity offering, insider selling, a split, media coverage.
- Community overlay: TradingView "Superstock 10-30 WMA Band" (10- and 30-period **weighted** MAs drawn as a band, styled after Chris Moody's slingshot, by UnknownUnicorn3844238 — the script page now returns 404; description seen in the TradingView script index). Note the book excerpt specifies a 10-week **simple** MA; "WMA" in community posts is ambiguous (weekly vs weighted).

## Evidence

### Self-reported (primary, unaudited)
- **$45,721 → $6,845,342** (14,972%) from **26 Sep 2003 to 27 Jan 2006**; the book's author bio says Oct 2003–Jan 2006 and that the S&P 500 returned **25%** over the same span. The documentation page posts monthly Ameritrade/TD Ameritrade statements, Gainskeeper capital-gains reports (2004–06) and IRS returns (2003–06), and states "A traditional CPA attestation could not be performed."
- Other self-reported bursts: +111% in 4 weeks, +117%/4 wk, +156%/8 wk, +264%/12 wk, +273%/16 wk, +371%/10 wk, +1,010%/17 wk, +1,026%/29 wk and +1,244%/46 wk (primary). Also the 61–106% drawdowns listed above, and an admission that most trades failed.
- No results after January 2006 are posted. He describes a 3½-year "trading retirement" before the 2013 book (primary).
- Context: the record window was a strong microcap bull market right after the 2002 low, and the first years of 2-business-day Form 4 reporting after Sarbanes-Oxley (Aug 2002, general knowledge, not fetched). One window, one account: it shows the method *can* produce extreme outcomes, not that it has positive expectancy.

### Independent tests
- **No independent backtest of the full method was found** (searches for Stine backtests/screeners and for 30-week-MA/Weinstein Stage-2 backtests returned only explainers). TradingSkeptic calls the magic-line idea "the definition of curve fitting" and says it is hard to separate skill from luck in a 28-month record.
- Anecdotal out-of-sample: tonyg99's Feb 2018 AOI checklist passed most technical items but had **no insider buying**. Alliance One was renamed Pyxus International in 2018 and filed Chapter 11 in 2020 (general knowledge, not fetched this run). It is one example, but it shows that technicals plus a low PE are not enough.

### Academic evidence on the components
- **Insider purchases.** Lakonishok & Lee (RFS 2001, NYSE/AMEX/Nasdaq 1975–1995): informativeness comes from **purchases, not sales**, and the cross-sectional predictability "is driven by insiders' ability to predict returns in smaller firms". Cohen, Malloy & Pomorski (JF 2012, 1986–2007): "opportunistic" (non-routine) insider trades earn value-weighted abnormal returns of **82 bp/month**, while routine trades earn ~0 (NBER Digest). Jeng, Metrick & Zeckhauser (REStat 2003) find purchases earn roughly 6%+ per year abnormal (from memory, not fetched). These results support Stine's insistence on multiple senior insiders buying in a meaningful size, but most of the alpha sits in small, illiquid names, the same place where costs are highest.
- **Moving-average timing in high-volatility stocks.** Han, Yang & Zhou (JFQA 2013): MA timing applied to volatility-sorted deciles "substantially outperform[s]" buy-and-hold, with the largest abnormal returns in the high-volatility deciles (portfolio-level, daily SMAs, before realistic microcap costs). This is indirect support for the 30-week MA in low-priced, volatile names.
- **Volume and 52-week highs** (from memory, not fetched): Gervais, Kaniel & Mingelgrin (JF 2001) find a high-volume return premium. George & Hwang (JF 2004) find that nearness to the 52-week high predicts returns. Both are consistent with the volume-thrust breakout.
- **Earnings.** Post-earnings-announcement drift is strongest in small, thinly followed firms (Bernard & Thomas 1989, from memory). This is consistent with "blockbuster earnings + no analyst coverage".
- **Base-rate warnings** (from memory, not fetched): lottery-like stocks with a low price, high idiosyncratic volatility and high MAX returns underperform on average (Bali, Cakici & Whitelaw, JFE 2011; Kumar, JF 2009). Bessembinder (JFE 2018) finds that ~4% of US stocks created all net wealth since 1926 while most underperform T-bills. "Superstocks" exist, but the unconditional base rate of the universe Stine trades is negative. Fama & French (JF 2008) find that anomalies are strongest in microcaps, which are a tiny share of market cap and expensive to trade.

### Reading the evidence
The individual ingredients (insider purchases in small caps, earnings surprises/drift, volume breakouts near highs, MA trend filters on volatile stocks) each have academic support. The specific combination and its numeric thresholds (float < 10M, PE run rate ≤ 10, < $15, 30-week MA, 10-week magic line, 7–10-week exits) are untested outside Stine's own 2003–06 record. Treat it as a hypothesis to backtest, not a validated system.

## Pitfalls
1. **Survivorship and hindsight.** The book's charts are mostly his winners, and the record is one 28-month window in a microcap bull market. The magic-line choice (which MA "the stock respects") is fitted after the fact.
2. **The title oversells insiders.** In the Lazy Man's Guide, insider buying is a *bonus*. Many Stine-style breakouts have none (AOI 2018). If you require it, the candidate count falls sharply.
3. **Microcap liquidity.** With a 4–8M float and a sub-$100M market cap, slippage, wide spreads and capacity limits dominate. A position that is a large share of weekly volume cannot be exited on a weekly signal without impact.
4. **Dilution.** Offerings and private placements are the #1 fundamental sell rule. In 2025–26, reverse splits, shelves/ATMs and warrant-laden follow-ons are pervasive in sub-$15, low-float names. New Nasdaq/NYSE rules effective 1 Jan 2026 limit using reverse splits to regain bid-price compliance (secondary, Substack, not verified against the exchange filings).
5. **PE run-rate trap.** Annualizing one blowout quarter can capture one-time gains, tax benefits or lumpy contracts. "Sustainable" and "operating leverage" are judgment calls.
6. **Pump overlap.** Low price, low float and promotional press releases also describe pump-and-dumps. The earnings, PE and insider-purchase filters are what separate them, so do not drop them.
7. **Weekly-bar latency and gaps.** Signals form at Friday's close. A weekly stop can be hit 20–40% below the trigger after an offering gap. Earnings are a deliberate entry catalyst, so holding through reports is part of the method.
8. **Drawdowns.** His early accounts fell 61–106%, his 2008 failure came from trading without stops, and he sized by Kelly. Copying his concentration without his stops is the failure mode he warns about.
9. **MA ambiguity.** Book excerpt: 10-week *simple*. Community scripts: 10/30 *weighted*. One TradingView idea uses a 20-week SMA. Fix one definition before testing.
10. **Undocumented market timing.** The "Canary in a Coalmine" crash indicator and the inflection-point calls are not specified anywhere accessible.

## 2025–2026 fit
- **Small-cap regime turned supportive.** The Russell 2000 set a closing record of 2,467.70 on 18 Sep 2025 after lagging large caps for most of 2025 (Investopedia via Yahoo, 19 Sep 2025). Through 31 Jul 2026 it was up ~**19% YTD**, beating the S&P 500 by ~**9 pp**, "one of the strongest opening runs of small-cap outperformance in the past 30 years" per Goldman, and beat the Russell 1000 by the widest first-half margin since 2001 (Integrity/VCM monthly commentary, July 2026). A search snippet (not fetched) reports a record close of 3,045.48 in August 2026, +22.7% YTD (unverified).
- **Leadership rotated toward Stine's side of the ledger.** AI-infrastructure names drove ~40% of the Russell 2000's mid-year return, then unwound in July 2026: the AI basket fell 22.4%, Russell 2000 Growth fell ~6% and the highest-P/E quintile fell 14.2%, while Russell 2000 Value was ~flat and up ~23% YTD. "Valuations started to matter again" (same source). A PE ≤ 10 earnings-winner screen fits a value-leaning small-cap tape better than the 2021 or H1-2026 momentum tape.
- **Caution on earnings.** Russell 2000 2026 EPS estimates were cut 9% YTD while S&P 500 estimates rose 9%, and about half of the small-cap YTD return came from multiple expansion. The commentary names Fed hikes, a flattening curve and a rising VIX as risks. Real blowout quarters will be scarcer than the index rally suggests.
- **Universe quality is worse.** The sub-$15, sub-10M-float pool in 2025–26 is crowded with recent micro-IPOs, reverse-split survivors and serial diluters (see `docs/smallcap-spec.md` red flags). Expect the PE/earnings/insider filters to reject most low-float runners.
- **Insider data is easier than in 2003.** Form 4 open-market purchases (code P) are free from EDGAR within two business days. In 2026, cluster buying is visible in beaten-down large caps (e.g., UnitedHealth directors in April 2026, per a search snippet, unverified), but that is a different setup from Stine's undiscovered microcaps.
- **Practitioner commentary.** The 2025–26 mentions (Jelinek Jan 2026, Das Nov 2025, hb_stocks Aug 2026) use Stine mainly for **sell rules and the magic line**, not as a full system. No 2025–26 performance study was found.
- **Verdict.** The regime is favourable (small caps leading, value rotation), but the investable universe is thin and dirty. Best use: a weekly overlay (30-week breakout plus magic-line pullback adds and Stine's sell rules) on the engine's existing insider-cluster and small-cap feeds, run at small size until a walk-forward test exists.

### Discretionary (cannot be fully coded; keep as Claude review enums or human steps)
- "Sustainable" earnings with "high operating leverage" and "easy comps"; super theme; "It Factor"; conservative management; clean chart. These belong in the `agent/review.py` layer with enumerated labels, not hard rules.
- Insider-buy quality (relative to the insider's wealth/salary, buying "all the way up").
- Which MA is the magic line for a given stock (fit risk).
- Market timing: inflection-point sentiment calls and the undefined "Canary in a Coalmine". Substitute `features/regime.py` (`market_trend_state`, `market_vol_regime`) plus a small-cap trend gate (e.g., IWM > its 30-week MA).
- Kelly-style concentration. The engine should keep `risk.risk_per_trade_pct` / `max_position_pct` caps instead.
- Selling "at $25" and media/message-board euphoria signals.
