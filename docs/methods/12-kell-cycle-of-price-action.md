# 12 — Kell Cycle of Price Action (Reversal Extension → Wedge Pop → EMA Crossback → Base n' Break → Exhaustion Extension → Wedge Drop)

*Practitioner write-up. Researched 2026-10-06 for swing-engine. Provenance tags: "(primary)" = Oliver Kell's own words
(his Substack, X posts, written interview answers, kelltrading.com); "(TraderLion)" = TraderLion articles and
"Anatomy of a Trade" pages produced with Kell — **these were behind a Cloudflare bot check this run and were read only
through search-engine snippets**, so wording is paraphrase, not verified quotation; "(secondary)" = third-party summaries;
"(unverified)" = could not be traced to Kell. The book *Victory in Stock Trading* (2021) and the paid courses were
not read.*

**One line.** Trade liquid, fast-growing leaders by placing them in a repeating price cycle measured against the
**daily 10- and 20-day EMAs** (with the 50/200-day SMAs and the 10/20-week EMAs as context): buy the first reclaim of
the EMAs after a capitulation (**Wedge Pop**), the first pullback that holds them (**EMA Crossback**) and the next
tight base that breaks out (**Base n' Break**); stop under the day's low / the EMA being bought; trail on the 10 or
20 EMA; sell into **extensions** far above the 10 EMA; get out on the **Wedge Drop** (first trade down through the
EMAs after an upside extension). Same cycle on the index (QQQ/SPX) sets exposure. Hold period about 2–12 weeks.

**Lineage.** O'Neil CAN SLIM (stock selection, base breakouts) plus Livermore/Darvas/Wyckoff cycle thinking (Kell
names all four — Stockbsessed interview, Apr 2023; Apple Books blurb). Kell won the **2020 U.S. Investing
Championship, stock division (accounts under $1M), +941.1%** (Business Wire final standings, 25 Jan 2021; Bowdoin
College news, Mar 2021). The cycle in one line, in his words (X, 6 Mar 2021, primary):
"Reversal Extension-->Wedge Pop-->EMA Crossback-->Base n Break-->(EMA Pullbacks...(more Base n Breaks in strong
trend))-->Exhaustive Extension-->Wedge Drop-->EMA Crossback-->Base N Break (down)-->Reversal Extension".

## Rules

### The cycle (definitions)
| Phase | Definition as taught | Role | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Reversal Extension | Downtrending stock pulls far away (below) its 10-day MA, capitulates at a low and snaps back, often on a volume surge; "fear takes over, and people finally sell" | Bottom; mostly a mindset signal; can be bought intraday near a daily key level | TraderLion "Reversal Extension: How Stocks Bottom" & "Cycle of Price Action" (snippets); Moglen interview notes 15 Oct 2021 |
| Wedge Pop ("the money pattern") | "First point where a stock rallies back through its moving averages after a Reversal Extension"; reclaims **both the 10- and 20-day EMAs** after a sharp drop, with rising accumulation volume; best after price tightens under/into the EMAs | First long entry of a new up-cycle | TraderLion Wedge Pop / Cycle pages (snippets); QuantVue definition "trades up through moving averages after reaching a downside extension" |
| EMA Crossback | First pullback to the 10- or 20-day EMA after the Wedge Pop; buy supportive action at the EMAs | Lowest-risk add / entry | TraderLion "EMA Crossback Uncovers Low-Risk Entries" (snippet) |
| Base n' Break | After the crossback, another pullback / sideways base into the 10/20 EMAs, "defined within just a couple of weeks", then a breakout on volume; repeats in strong trends | Continuation entry; used "to add to leading stocks" | TraderLion "Base n' Break: How to Catch Breakouts" (snippet); Kell X 6 Mar 2021 |
| Exhaustion Extension | Euphoric blow-off that extends price "well above the moving averages on both the higher and lower time frames", usually with heavy (churning) volume | Sell into strength | TraderLion (snippet); Moglen 2021: extended from the 10 EMA on **both daily and weekly** = needs to base |
| Wedge Drop | "First time a stock trades down through the moving averages after reaching an upside extension" | Exit longs / short entry | TraderLion "Wedge Drop: How to Sell Short" (snippet); QuantVue |
| Down-cycle | Downside EMA Crossback (rally into declining EMAs) and Downside Base n' Break, ending in the next Reversal Extension | Short side / stay out | TraderLion downside articles (snippets) |

Cycle character: "The up cycles last a bit longer while the down cycles are quicker and sloppier" (Moglen paraphrasing
Kell, Trading Engineered, 15 Jan 2023). The framework is fractal — the same phases are read on weekly, daily and
65-minute charts (Moglen 2021; Kell Substack 2023 uses it on QQQ weekly/daily and NVDA 65-min).

### Market filter
Kell runs the same cycle on the index and breadth (all primary unless noted):
- **Index cycle = buy/sell signal.** QQQ (growth bellwether), also SPY/SPX and the equal-weight QQQE. "QQQ Daily — Treating
  the scum as the Exhaustion Extension ending the down cycle and Friday's Wedge Pop as triggering a new daily up cycle"
  (Substack, 4 Mar 2023). "The hammer into the broadening line had the potential to be the Reversal Extension and that
  is confirmed with the Wedge Pop Friday. Treating us as in an up cycle until further notice" (7 Oct 2023).
- **Index below the 20 EMA = correction mode.** "Buying in pieces, not trading or reducing size during index
  corrections (below 20EMA) ... are the key elements to keep me out of trouble" (interview, Apr 2023). March 2025:
  "In studying 2018/2022 market when got through the 20ema was when things reversed" (X, 24 Mar 2025).
- **Sell-signal regime = no long swings.** "I am continuing to avoid long swings until I see a major capitulation
  reversal extension in the QQQ or we switch to a buy signal" (Substack, 23 Sep 2023).
- **Breadth (secondary indicator): NASI** (Nasdaq Summation Index). "To flip positive, we need to see NASI flip black
  with CONFIRMATION above the 10EMA"; "I often have the most success when we are in the black trending above the
  10EMA" (Sep/Oct 2023). "Black/red" is his chart colouring; the exact colouring rule is **not published**.
- **Index extension.** QQQ "4-5% above the Daily 10EMA ... has signaled cooling off/basing in the past" (4 Mar 2023).
- **Exposure by environment.** Good: "6-7 core positions and will go on margin". Poor: "only trade 1-2 stocks and cap
  my exposure at 30%. If I can't make progress I will reduce further." Late in the cycle: "learning to just sit out or
  pass on a setup" (Apr 2023).
- **Self-sentiment tell.** Getting negative at the lows "has been a REALLY STRONG indicator for me in the past"
  (4 Mar 2023).
- Third-party simplification: "QQQ below 20-EMA = caution, above = green light, below 50-SMA = major warning; margin
  only when index > 20-EMA" (GitHub rulebook, unverified).

### Entry
- **Where.** Buy "in the pocket" — at/near the 10/20 EMA or a tight pivot — never extended. From his trade-journal
  data: "nearly all my losers took place buying things too extended from the moving averages ... It may only be 3-4%
  above the moving averages but that was too much" (primary, Apr 2023). Taking a small "plan to add" position when
  slightly extended "has never worked for me" (primary).
- **Wedge Pop.** Wait for tightness under/into the EMAs, then act on a strong, volume-backed bar through the 10/20
  EMAs and the pivot (TraderLion). Examples: MU gapped up "in the form of a Wedge Pop through the moving averages over
  the previous flag formation"; AMD triggered after an earnings reaction reclaimed **all** its MAs including the
  200-day SMA (TraderLion Anatomy of a Trade, snippets).
- **EMA Crossback.** Buy the first pullback that holds the 10 or 20 EMA; "the best case scenario for me is buying
  support on the daily chart via a breakout on the 65 Min chart" (primary).
- **Base n' Break.** Base of roughly 1–3 weeks riding the 10/20 EMAs, buy the break of the base/pivot on volume
  (TraderLion). Also used for adds.
- **Execution timeframe.** 65-minute chart for entries/adds ("pullbacks on the 65 Min chart for adds and often even
  initial positions"); watches 10-/15-min only early; "do not trade the first 10-15 minutes unless a stock has a
  catalyst like earnings"; later screens = weekly, daily, 195-min, 65-min (primary).
- **Alerts.** Set "around the 10/20EMA or a breakout level" on all owned, Main and A-list names (primary).
- **Two pieces.** Example given: support buy at the 20 EMA = **15% position with a 1–2% stop (15–30 bps of account at
  risk)**; if it follows through next day add another **15% → 30% position**, raising the stop to the follow-through
  day's low, keeping total risk ~20–30 bps (primary). "Never average down: Average Up" (Top-5 rules, primary).

### Stop
- Breakout buy: "the breakout pivot or day's low". Support buy: "against the 10 or 20EMA and will use that low as my
  stop" (primary). "If buying a breakout and it doesn't go I will just sell it now without hesitation" (primary).
- Staggered stops: MU — stops staggered at the 10 and 20 EMA under the day's low (TraderLion snippet). AMD — new add
  stopped at the low of the new breakout area; original lot raised to the prior "ignite bar" (TraderLion snippet).
- Typical initial stop distance 1–2% for support buys (primary example). Secondary write-ups say 1–3% (EBC, unverified).
- Losses are exits of "the reason for buying is no longer present"; a stock breaking the 20 EMA is the usual stop
  (primary). Same stock stopped 2–3× while still consolidating = buy criteria not specific enough (primary).

### Exits and targets
- **No preset targets.** "I do not use pre-determined targets but more base it on the length of the move and how the
  stock is trading relative to the moving averages" (primary).
- **Trail on the 10/20 EMA.** "If a stock has been trending for multiple weeks it will usually let you know which
  moving average is of importance and that is the one I will use" (primary). FWTV summary: closes below both the 10
  and 20 EMA end the up-trend (secondary).
- **Sell into extensions** from the daily 10 EMA (primary); extended from the 10 EMA on daily **and** weekly = expect a
  base (Moglen 2021). Kell's published numbers are index/large-cap only: QQQ 4–5% above the daily 10 EMA (cooling),
  NVDA "12-15% above the 10 Week" (extended) (Substack, Mar 2023). He publishes **no universal % threshold**.
- **Wedge Drop = out.** AMD: after ~50%, resistance at a broadening line, a gap down through the MAs — exited on the
  Wedge Drop (TraderLion snippet).
- Third-party mechanisations (all **unverified** as Kell's rules): QuantVue — extension when the bar's low is > 1.5×ATR
  above the 10 EMA (high < 1.5×ATR below for downside); vishnuv script v8.0 — ADR-scaled, ~5% daily / 7–8% weekly /
  ~10% capitulation from the 10 EMA, second exhaustion bar = SELL; GitHub rulebook — 5%+ above daily 10 EMA or 15%+
  weekly, "1st extension hold, 2nd take 50%, 3rd exit", "at 5x risk sell 50%".

### Position sizing
- Primary (Apr 2023): sizing driven by **account exposure** and how well he is trading; 6–7 core positions on margin in
  good tape; 1–2 names capped at 30% exposure in bad tape; initial pieces 15% with 1–2% stops (15–30 bps risk per
  piece), up to ~30% per name after the follow-through add.
- FWTV summary (Aug 2021, secondary) of his championship-era book: 8–12 positions, ~190% gross (≈2:1 margin) — two best
  ideas 35% each, next two 20%, next four 12%, last four 8%.
- EBC / GitHub rulebook (unverified): top ideas 25–35% on margin, net exposure 120–150%, risk 1–2% per trade. The 1–2%
  figure conflicts with Kell's own 15–30 bps per-piece example; prefer the primary.
- Progressive exposure: "I buy in pieces and add additional positions as the original positions work. I can go fully
  invested probably a lot more quickly than most people and this can hurt me" (primary).

### Holding period
"All my answers led to the intermediate term timeframe (2-12 weeks give or take)"; he stopped trying to be a
"multi-earnings cycle position trader" and does not day trade (primary, Apr 2023). Failed entries are cut the same or
next day; winners ride the 10/20 EMA until an extension sale or a Wedge Drop.

### Shorts (out of scope for long-only swing-engine)
Mirror image: short the Wedge Drop, the Downside EMA Crossback into declining EMAs, and the Downside Base n' Break; cover
into a Reversal Extension (TraderLion downside articles). Kell: shorting is harder; he covers quickly (Substack 2023).

## Chart signatures
1. **Reversal Extension:** after a down-cycle, a wide-range capitulation bar (often a gap down or undercut of a
   prior low / 200-day / trendline) far below a declining 10 EMA, heavy volume, close off the lows (hammer); on the
   index often coincides with extreme bearish sentiment.
2. **Wedge (the coil):** price stops falling, ranges tighten, the 10 EMA flattens and converges with the 20 EMA; price
   sits just under/inside the EMA cluster (a falling wedge / flag into the MAs). Volume dries up.
3. **Wedge Pop bar:** gap or wide-range up bar that closes above both the 10 and 20 EMAs and the wedge pivot, volume
   above average ("accumulation"). Post-earnings reclaims of all MAs (incl. the 200-day) are the strongest variant.
4. **EMA Crossback:** 2–5-bar pullback on lighter volume that tags the now-rising 10/20 EMA and holds; a 65-minute
   breakout off that support is the trigger.
5. **Base n' Break:** 1–3 weeks of sideways, tight action letting the EMAs "catch up"; breakout bar on volume; in strong
   trends several stacked Base n' Breaks ("staircase" along the 10 EMA).
6. **Exhaustion Extension:** parabolic run / gap far above the 10 EMA on both daily and weekly, climactic volume or
   churning (high volume, little progress).
7. **Wedge Drop:** first close (often a gap) down through the 10/20 EMAs after the extension; subsequent rallies fail at
   the declining EMAs (Downside Crossback).
8. **Index overlay:** QQQ above a rising 20 EMA with NASI rising above its 10 EMA = full exposure; QQQ below the 20 EMA
   = reduce / pieces only.

- Kell reports ~10 years of trading, unprofitable for the first six, then annual gains of roughly 70–90% before 2020
  (Bowdoin news, via search snippet — **unaudited**). No published year-by-year record after 2020 was found.
- His own trade-journal finding (TraderSync tagging): losers cluster in entries 3–4%+ above the MAs (primary). Useful
  qualitative evidence for the "buy in the pocket" rule; no statistics published.

### Independent / mechanical tests
- **None found** for the full cycle or for Wedge Pop / EMA Crossback specifically (searches across TradingView,
  Substack, GitHub, Reddit, quant blogs, Oct 2026). The TradingView scripts publish signals, not performance. The
  GitHub rulebook's "65–75% win rate" style numbers have no backtest behind them.
- Nearest proxy: a plain 20-EMA timing rule on SPY returned **3.06% CAGR vs 7.87% buy-and-hold** (QuantifiedStrategies,
  "20 EMA Trading Strategy", via snippet) — the EMA reclaim alone, on an index, is not an edge; Kell's claimed edge is in
  stock selection (growth leaders), tightness filters, sizing and exits.

### Academic backdrop (supports pieces, not the package)
- **Trend / MA distance:** Avramov, Kaplanski & Subrahmanyam, "Moving Average Distance as a Predictor of Equity
  Returns", *Review of Financial Economics* 39(2), 2021, 127–145: the 21-day/200-day MA ratio predicts cross-sectional
  returns, ~9% annual value-weighted alpha, stronger on the long side, beyond momentum and 52-week-high effects
  (US stocks > $5, 1977–2018). Supports trading stocks whose short MAs sit above long MAs (the "EMA stack").
- **52-week-high proximity:** George & Hwang (2004, *J. Finance*) — supports Kell's 52-week-high scan (from prior
  docs, not re-fetched).
- **Short-term reversal:** Jegadeesh (1990) and Lehmann (1990) — 1-week/1-month winners underperform — consistent with
  "don't buy extended, sell extensions, buy pullbacks to the MA" (from knowledge, not re-fetched).
- **Momentum crashes:** Daniel & Moskowitz (2016, *JFE*; NBER w20439) — after bear markets, high-volatility rebounds
  favour past losers; the index Reversal Extension → Wedge Pop window is exactly where momentum longs crash. Kell's
  answer is to buy the RS leaders that held up and to size in pieces; this tension is untested.

### Reading the evidence
The method is a discretionary, well-specified *vocabulary* layered on known effects (trend, 52-week highs,
short-term reversal at extremes). The one hard number (+941%) is a single-year, margin-assisted contest result. Treat
any mechanical version as a new hypothesis that must clear docs/gates.md walk-forward tests.

## Pitfalls
1. **Undefined thresholds.** "Extension", "tight", "wedge" and "pocket" have no published numbers beyond index
   examples; third-party codings disagree (1.5×ATR vs 5% vs ADR-scaled). Pick one, version it, never tune per trade.
2. **Whipsaw at the EMAs.** Kell's own TSLA 65-min example (Apr 2023): four trades — two scratches, two losers — before
   one held. Expect clusters of small losses around the 10/20 EMA in chop.
3. **Buying extended.** His data: entries 3–4% above the MAs produced nearly all losers. Mechanical scans that fire on
   the breakout bar close often violate this.
4. **False index Wedge Pops.** Bear-market rallies reclaim the 20 EMA too (2018, 2022). Kell posted a "wedge pop buy
   signal for my strategy" on 24 Mar 2025; the "Liberation Day" crash followed (S&P 500 −10.5% from the 2 Apr close
   to the 4 Apr close; QQQ low $402.39 on 7 Apr 2025). The stop rules contain this; blind exposure does not.
5. **Leverage.** Championship-era sizing ran ~190% gross on margin with 35% top positions (FWTV). Copying that without
   his win/loss asymmetry is a ruin risk; swing-engine caps (10% per position, 1% risk) are far tighter by design.
6. **Survivorship / contest selection.** USIC winners are the right tail of many entrants; the case-study pages are
   winners (+20% to +110%).
7. **Earnings gaps.** Several showcased trades are post-earnings reclaims; holding into earnings means stops can gap.
8. **Execution timeframe.** The low-risk property comes from 65-minute triggers against daily support. A daily-bar
   version buys later and wider; expect worse R.
9. **Third-party drift.** Scribd "Gemini" tutorials and the GitHub rulebook add invented probabilities and rules; cite
   only Kell / TraderLion.
10. **Overfitting when coding the state machine.** Phase labels depend on lookbacks (what counts as "the" extension,
    "first" crossback); small changes reshuffle signals. Log every variant in research/trials.py.

### Mechanical (can be coded on daily bars; feature gaps flagged)
| Element | swing-engine mapping |
