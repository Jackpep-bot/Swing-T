Signals

Every idea the strategies produced, including the ones the system did not trade, with the reason it skipped them and Claude's review. Numbers come from the strategy code; the review only gates whether an entry goes ahead.

Shadow ledger

The shadow ledger grades every signal, taken or not, against the bars that followed (next-day open entry, first touch of stop or target), so you can see which strategies actually work without waiting for real trades. Results are in R: 1R is the planned risk per share.

n = graded signals (taken and not taken alike). Expectancy = average R per graded signal. Profit factor = gross wins / gross losses. Rows with fewer than 20 graded signals are dimmed: too few to judge. Pending = not resolved yet. Live vs replay drift

Each strategy's live shadow ledger over the recent window next to its replay, both net of round-trip slippage, in R per traded signal. A flag means the live average sits outside the replay average ± 2 standard errors (only once there are at least 20 live trades at that horizon): below = the strategy is doing worse live than it did in replay.

n = live trades at the horizon (signals that were entered and resolved). Win = share of those with positive net R. Live / Replay = average net R. Diff = live − replay. Rows without a replay ledger in this store show dashes.

Kill switch

Tripping the kill switch creates state/KILL. While it exists the autopilot refuses every new entry, close and stop change. The only thing it still does is cancel resting unfilled entries. Stop and target legs already at the broker keep protecting your positions; the monitor and the nightly data jobs keep running.

Trip it when you do not understand a fill, the equity is not what the journal says, a feed is dead during market hours while you hold positions, or you are travelling.

Clearing it is deliberately not possible from here. Write the cause in the journal, then on the computer run rm state/KILL followed by uv run swing autopilot --dry-run.
